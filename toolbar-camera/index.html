Explorer's Toolbar CameraWR1-303-IA01 · prototype (Figma “Toolbar Mockup”, page v3 · 16/11), WR1 colours

Notes
  1. Navigation review version (23/11): the same flow and variants as the main prototype, to check the navigation. The attract screen is still: no whale animation, and the circles that mark the RFID reader on the right do not pulse.
  2. 0 · Attract screen (23/11): the piece waits here until someone starts it. In the Media Brief it starts with the wristband (RFID) or by tapping the screen; in this prototype, tap the screen or press the shutter. The call to action is the element of the Guano Pile attract screen as it is: peach text aligned right and three concentric circles on the right edge that mark the RFID reader (here the circles light up from the inside out, proposal). Text: “Scan your wristband or tap the screen to start” (in Guano Pile, “Scan your wristband to start”, with the Arabic below, not included here). It uses the WR1 gallery style of the Guano Pile attract screen (KTF proposal): night-blue background, rounded triangles with a thick peach border (the big one shows the whale model) and the piece name as title (provisional).
  3. Attract screen with the whole scene (proposal, 24/11): by default, the attract screen shows the camera image of the whole whale model, not blurred and zoomed out so most of it fits, with a slow pan from side to side (18 s). The blue dots pulse on the animals and the fish swim across, so visitors see what happens in the piece before they start. Night-blue shades at the top and bottom keep the title and the call to action readable. Under Variants, the WR1 gallery style with triangles. In the real piece this would be a looping video or animation, as the camera is not moving on its own.
  4. Attract animation (proposal, 23/11): a whale drawn like the triangles (night-blue fill and peach border) swims out of the big triangle (gallery style only): it starts small and far inside it, comes closer, crosses its bottom-left side in front of the border and leaves the screen. It loops every 9 s. Under Variants, without the animation. A version with the whale of the render coming out of the frame would need a clean cut-out of the model or a video.
  5. 1 · Move the camera: after the attract screen, “Move the camera to explore”, now just under the icon (23/11), over the camera image, blurred and darkened (night blue at 45%). As in the video, the camera stays still and an icon of the device rocks from side to side to invite visitors to move it. Four icon options under Variants: three based on the front elevation of the device drawing (outline, on its post with the head tilting, and solid with the shutter button; the button position is a guess) and the one from the video. As soon as someone moves the camera, it goes to the instructions.
  6. 2 · Instructions: the viewfinder with the four arrows moving in a wave, and “Centre an dot and press”, the text from the Figma (16/11), styled like “Move the camera to explore” but in bold. If you reach a rotation stop, the arrow on that side turns into the straight line.
  7. 3 · Detected: a dot counts as soon as it is less than 50 px from the centre. The reticle turns orange, the dot pops and sends out a ripple, the arrows disappear and the ring fills in 0.8 s (dwell). If you move off, the arrows come back. When the ring is full, it moves to “Ready” on its own.
  8. 4 · Ready: the “Press the shutter” prompt in the dark brown pill (gallery-accent/950) with light text (gallery-accent/50). By default (23/11) it is bigger, right next to the circle (upper right) and has an animated arrow pointing to the physical shutter button; under Variants it can go back to the bottom centre.
  9. Shutter: in the piece it is a physical button, the mechanical part of the camera. Here it is the round button next to the screen, or the space bar. It takes the photo when the camera is ready and closes the species card. A shutter sound would play.
  10. Shutter with nothing in focus (proposal, 23/11): pressing the shutter before a dot is in the viewfinder and the ring is full shows an important message in the centre of the screen, over a dark overlay. It disappears after 2 s. Three wordings to compare under Variants.
  11. Direction arrows (proposals, 23/11): by default the four arrows only appear after 3 s without moving the camera. Other options under Variants: only until the camera is first moved, only the straight line when a rotation stop is reached, or always (as before).
  12. Guide to the nearest dot (proposal, 23/11): while exploring with no dot in the viewfinder, a glowing white arrow (like the direction arrows) just outside the circle points to the nearest dot that has not been photographed yet, with a gentle back-and-forth. Under Variants it can also show a dotted line to the dot, or be turned off. It hides as soon as a dot is detected.
  13. 5 · Photo: white flash. By default there is no message (23/11); under Variants, “Got it” can appear centred on the screen. Then the image blurs, the viewfinder disappears and the dots fade.
  14. 6 · Species card (Figma v3, 16/11): hexagon with the photo and a peach border, and a night-blue panel with a pointed end. Name and scientific name in peach, description in white. Below the card, “Press the shutter to close” in the same pill as “Press the shutter”, with an arrow to the physical button (23/11; before, “Press the button to close”). The background is blurred and darkened at 40%. It closes with the shutter.
  15. 7 · Fish: after the first card closes, a school of fish swims in from the left and keeps swimming, anchored to the room. They cannot be photographed. The arrows and the instruction no longer appear, and the photographed animal stays small and dimmed.
  16. Dots: four 24 px dots, electric blue with an almost white centre and a blue halo (reference image, 17/11), one per species from the video (Squat lobster, Black hagfish, Ghostly grenadier and Snow crab), each with its own text.
  17. Review comments (24/11, proposal): under Variants, by default, the viewfinder is only the circle, without the lines in the centre, so it does not look so much like shooting; and a dot that has already been photographed stays smaller and still (no pulse) but is no longer faded, so it can still be seen. While this option is on, it sets the Reticle. “As before” brings back the lines and the faded dots.
  18. Review comments (16/11), now fixed: the viewfinder lens neither blurs nor magnifies; the UI has no shadows (ring, reticle, arrows, texts and pills; the dots keep their colour glow); the dot is electric blue. “Circle only, no crosshair” is an option under Reticle. Since 23/11 the dwell is fixed at 0.8 s, the detection zone at 50 px and the viewfinder circle is 30% smaller than in the Figma; the only background is the whale model, the attract screen uses the gallery style and screen 1 the device icon.
  19. Review: “← Back”, “Next →” and “Go to” take you to each moment of the flow. It waits on the attract screen until you tap the screen or press the shutter. “Go to” plays the flow on its own from the chosen screen; dragging the image, Back/Next or the shutter stop it. “Reset” goes back to the attract screen. Keyboard arrows: back and next. The tab at the top (“Hide controls”) hides this bar, with the variants and these notes, to see the screen bigger; “Show controls” brings it back.

Everything is a proposal. Texts from the video and from Montse's Figma (v3, 16/11). Provisional: the card photo is the moray eel from the reference for every species. WR1 gallery colours from ILMI World DS Lite (KTF proposal). Typeface: Roboto in this web copy (the Ilmi, a KTF proposal, is not published), at the Figma sizes. Background: the whale model, which is what this piece scans (render of the room from the WR1-303 30% Media Brief, enlarged, hence soft; the dots sit on the whale). The cards still use the video texts (provisional). The language selection that the Media Brief (60%) and the video put after the start is not included. The round shutter button is a simulation of the physical button; its look is a proposal.
